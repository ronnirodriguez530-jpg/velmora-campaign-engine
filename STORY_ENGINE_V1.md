# Velmora Story Engine V1

## Purpose

Velmora is a replayable campaign engine, not a prewritten campaign book. The user establishes and approves protected canon. The engine is responsible for turning that foundation into a coherent long-form campaign during play.

The user must not be required to author every scene, quest, NPC response, or branch before playing.

## Division of responsibility

### User-approved foundation

- Protected world canon and hard boundaries
- Faction identities and foundational conflicts
- The fixed opening event and permanent d6 starting situations
- Stage-gated truths such as the First Speaker's hidden transformation
- Content exclusions and major changes to established canon

### Engine-generated campaign content

- The immediate crisis and first playable objective
- Scene framing, dialogue, complications, and transitions
- Main, faction, and side questlines assembled for the current campaign
- NPC identities, motives, decisions, relationships, memories, and reactions
- Clues, rumors, failures, recovery routes, and consequences
- Variable replacement-Speaker candidates and political events
- Pacing, callbacks, quiet moments, escalation, and delayed payoffs
- Campaign-specific ordering and combination of approved threats, creatures, relics, locations, and faction pressures

## Required operating layers

### 1. Protected canon

The engine loads facts and boundaries it may not rewrite. Generated content must be checked against this layer before it becomes durable campaign state.

### 2. Campaign blueprint generator

At campaign creation, the engine uses the campaign seed and approved content pools to create a hidden campaign blueprint. The blueprint contains stage-appropriate pressures, faction agendas, candidate story threads, clue routes, possible reversals, and multiple valid resolution directions. It is not a fixed screenplay and may be revised when player choices invalidate an anticipated route.

The blueprint must never select Resolution-stage possession, permanent control of the First Speaker, or another endgame event during an earlier stage.

### 3. Story-thread ledger

The engine persistently tracks open questions, promises, clues, threats, relationships, faction agendas, player-created goals, unresolved consequences, and dormant threads. Each thread records its origin, current state, involved entities, stage limits, urgency, last use, and possible recovery routes.

This ledger is the campaign's story memory. It prevents the Campaign Master from treating each scene as an isolated prompt.

The Campaign Master may activate, advance, block, resolve, fail, or replace retrieved threads only through validated tools. A replacement must follow a recovery path already recorded on the source thread and inherits that thread's visibility and stage range. This prevents adaptation from becoming an unrelated shortcut, a secret leak, or premature escalation.

### 4. Scene and quest composer

Before a scene, the engine retrieves only the relevant canon, current state, NPC knowledge, active story threads, player history, and approved reference principles. The Campaign Master creates the playable scene from that packet.

Quests are generated from validated structures with an objective, stakes, involved parties, discoverable information, failure conditions, recovery routes, and durable consequences. They do not require prewritten prose or a single preset solution.

### 5. NPC behavior

NPC actions come from their own motives, knowledge, memories, relationships, faction pressures, and current circumstances. NPCs must not know hidden campaign truth merely because the Campaign Master knows it. Important NPC decisions and changes are persisted through validated engine tools.

### 6. Adaptation and learning

The engine observes play rather than retraining a model during a campaign. It records usable signals such as player-chosen approaches, preferred pacing, recurring interests, abandoned threads, repetition, scene length, combat frequency, and requested tone.

Those signals may adjust retrieval, pacing, and content selection. Adaptation cannot rewrite canon, secretly change rules, or grant itself new authority. Behavioral changes must be evaluated before becoming defaults.

### 7. Reference learning

The engine may use an approved, expandable library of public-domain, openly licensed, user-owned, or original sources. It retrieves distilled principles and structural examples rather than copying scenes, characters, settings, or distinctive language.

Reference material teaches techniques such as mystery construction, faction pressure, quest variation, NPC motivation, consequence design, pacing, and recovery after failure. Free browsing during live play is not required and must not silently introduce unverified canon.

## Replayability rule

Replayability comes from recombining approved world material under different campaign seeds, opening crises, faction agendas, NPC identities, clue routes, consequences, and player choices. The fixed First Speaker attack remains the common inciting event, not a fixed sequence of scenes.

## Minimum authored content rule

Hand-authored material is required only when it protects identity, safety, coherence, or mechanical validation. A content gap must not automatically become a request for the user to write a scene. First determine whether the missing piece belongs in protected canon, an approved content pool, a reusable generator, or the persistent story ledger.

## V1 success test

V1 succeeds when several campaigns can begin from different d6 starts and hidden opening crises, produce different coherent quest paths, remember consequences and NPC behavior, avoid premature endgame escalation, recover from failed or unexpected player actions, and remain inside protected Velmora canon without the user prewriting scenes.
